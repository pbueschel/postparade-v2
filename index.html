The problem

Placing a horse takes a whole afternoon.

Pulling condition books off four different track sites. Cross-checking eligibility clause by clause. Comparing purse and distance. Getting a rider named. Then typing it all into the entry box before it closes — and it is all manual.

  • Condition language is dense and inconsistent across tracks
  • One missed preference tier and your entry does not draw in
  • A decline arrives with no reason attached to the record
  • The right spot at another track on your circuit gets missed entirely
How it works

Four steps, and no black box in the middle.

The software handles the paperwork and reads the conditions. A person at the racing office makes every call.

1

The track publishes its book

Every race, its purse, surface, distance, conditions as written, and how many spots it holds.

2

You read it and submit

Filter by entry close, race date, surface, type, or purse. Name a jockey, flag AE or MTO, submit.

3

The conditions get checked

Sex, age, the maiden and non-winners ladders, claiming clauses, registry, the vet's list.

4

The office accepts or declines

A standard reason plus a comment. You see the reason, the comment, and the timestamp.

The record

Every submission and decision is an append-only row. The trainer view, the office view, and the system activity log all read the same ones.

  • Submitted, decided, and by whom
  • Decline reason plus comment, stored
  • Spot counts identical on both ends
  • Raw submission records, viewable
Features

Built around the work you already do.

Eligibility

Every condition, checked.

Age, sex, prior wins, claiming and starter history, state-bred restrictions, breed registry, preference clauses — all read against the horse's record. If they meet it, you know. If they do not, you know which clause and why.

  • Shows the clause next to the pass or the flag — auditable, not magic
  • Flags a conflict; it never rules on one
  • “Submit anyway” always available, with the flags snapshotted onto the record

Conditions, structured

Condition books, in one place.

Condition text becomes structured fields — type, distance, surface, purse, sex and age, the ladder, the clauses — so a card can be sorted and filtered instead of read four times. The raw text stays one click away, because you may read a clause differently than the checker does.

  • “See all conditions” shows the book's own words, verbatim
  • Filter by entry close, race date, surface, type, or purse
  • Extras added after the book publishes show up in the bell
Records & riders

Every step timestamped, on both ends.

A jockey is named at submission, from a rider list with agents, so the office can catch a double-booking before the box closes. Submitted, decided, by whom — the trainer view, the office view, and the system log read the same append-only rows.

  • A rider is required at submission
  • Declines carry a standard reason plus a comment
  • AE and MTO designations carry through to the overnight

The revision

What changed from v1

The first build put a recommendation engine at the centre. This one takes it out and puts the condition book there instead.

v1v2Why
Fit scores, acceptance likelihood, draw-in probability A condition checker that flags conflicts Eligibility is a rule question. Outcome is not our call.
Recommendations screen See condition books → select track → race list Trainers read the book. The app gets them to it faster.
“Spot alerts” and a dashboard block of closing entries One Entry windows page The capability stays; it stops being pushed at you.
Suggested ships, win rates, speed and class figures Records and past performances, as facts Nothing statistical about a future or past result.
Horses tab deep-linked to one horse A real roster for trainers; entries and history for tracks It was a bug, and the tab had no defined contents.
Requests inbox A submissions log, trainer → track Submissions are the record that needs storing.
No messaging Stored threads between office and barn Most placement questions are a conversation.
Hover to act on a horse row The underlined horse name is the link, always Hover did not fire on horses already placed.
Thin horse pages Equibase-shaped profiles Color, sex, state bred, year foaled, sire and dam, connections of last start.

About the data

Real where it is cited: trainer, horses, sires and dams, owners, and the results referenced in each profile. Illustrative where it must be: the condition books, race cards, spot counts, messages, and the demo calendar, which rolls forward off the clock so the books are always open. Nothing in here is a rating or a projection, and no number claims to be one. This is a concept prototype, not a system of record.
